Who was Gilgamesh?

Gilgamesh, known in Sumerian as Bilgamesh (𒀭𒄑𒉋𒂵𒎌), was a Sumerian hero known for his adventures with Enkidu (𒂗𒆠𒄭), and his attempt to find eternal life. According to poetry, Gilgamesh was the son of a human named Lugalbanda (𒈗𒌉𒁕, meaning Fierce King) who was Gilgamesh's predecessor, and Ninsun (𒀭𒊩𒌆𒄢, Lady of the wild cows), the goddess of wild cows.

The Epic of Gilgamesh

The Epic of Gilgamesh is a series of clay tablets written in the Sumerian Language. However, Akkadian translations were written by Sîn-lēqi-unninni (𒁹𒀭𒌍𒋾𒀀𒅆). The Epic of Gilgamesh is the oldest written text in human history, predating the Bible, Torah, Qur'an, Iliad, Odyssey, Aeneid, and many other ancient texts, and was the inspiration for Hercules. The epic is about Gilgamesh/Bilgamesh, the tyranical king of Uruk (𒀕𒆠). He ruled Uruk with an iron fist. The gods created Enkidu (𒂗𒆠𒄭), a wild creature made to humble Gilgamesh. After becoming civilised through sexual initiation with Shamhat, Enkidu headed to Uruk to find Gilgamesh. After losing the battle against Gilgamesh, the two became best friends and take a 6-day journey to the Cedar Forest to slay it's guardian, Humbaba. Together, Gilgamesh and Enkidu slay Humbaba and cut down the sacred cedar. Ishtar, the goddess of love, war, and sex sent the Bull of Heaven to kill Gilgamesh and Enkidu. However, the Bull of Heaven was slain by the duo, and Ishtar was insulted. This led to Enkidu being punished by the gods, recieving a fatal illness. See More

Summary

The epic is about Gilgamesh who takes a journey with Enkidu, and later tries to find the secret to eternal life.

The Lesson

The Epic of Gilgamesh teaches that friendship is something we as humans should value. It also teaches how we as humans are limited to what our bodies allow us to do, even Gilgamesh who was a demigod, could not break his human limitations and become immortal. As true immortality is not about living for millions of years, it is the legacy you leave behind; your impact on the world before you die.

Real, or Fiction?

Many Assyriologists (people who study Mesopotamian history) agree that Gilgamesh was a real king during the Early Dynastic Period of Mesopotamian history. An era where cuneiform (the writing system Sumerian, Akkadian, Hittite, and Old Persian) was starting to emerge as logograms (letters that represent words).
